# Sources

What each source can and cannot support. Consulted 2026-09-03.

## Encyclopedic

**Wikipedia — Bishnoi** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnoi
Supports: Jambheshwar's dates (1451–1536), Peepasar, Samrathal Dhora 1485, Mukam,
120 shabads / Shabadwani, the 29 niyamas and their conventional four-way
categorisation, ~360 gotras of equal standing, caste origins, geographic spread.
Does not support: any competing etymology — the article gives only the *bis + nau*
derivation. Internally inconsistent on population (~1,500,000 in the lead versus
"as of 2010, an estimated 600,000"), so cannot settle that number alone.

**Wikipedia — Khejarli massacre** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khejarli_massacre
Supports: September 1730, Abhai Singh of Marwar, minister Giridhar Bhandari
leading the party, the rejected bribe, the sequence (elders first), 363 killed,
Bishnois travelling from 83 villages to Jehnad, the apology, and the decree —
which it states narrowly, as an exemption for that village from supplying wood.
Hedges the purpose of the timber: "some sources report" palace construction,
"others note" lime-burning. Names "three of her daughters" without naming them.

**Wikipedia — Amrita Devi** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrita_Devi
**Wikipedia — Amrita Devi Bishnoi National Award** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amrita_Devi_Bishnoi_National_Award
Supports: the award's existence and purpose — recognition for courage or work in
wildlife protection. The clearest documented "past becomes present" link available.

## Community and popular

**Sahapedia — "When Amrita Devi and 362 Bishnois sacrificed their lives for the Khejri tree"**
http://www.sahapedia.org/when-amrita-devi-and-362-bishnois-sacrificed-their-lives-khejri-tree
Note the framing in the title: Amrita Devi **and 362 others**, totalling 363.
Useful for narrative detail. Community-facing rather than peer-reviewed — grade B.

**Bishnoi Village Safari — About Bishnoi** · http://www.bishnoivillagesafari.org/bishnoi_village_safari/aboutbishnoi.html
**Jatland / Jat Chiefs — Amrita Devi** · https://www.jatland.com/home/Amrita_Devi · https://jatchiefs.com/amrita-devi/
Corroborate the daughters' names (Asu, Ratni, Bhagu) and the 49-villages figure.
Community sources; sufficient for a grade B attributed statement, not for an
unattributed one.

**Vajiram & Ravi — Bishnoi Movement; National Forest Martyrs Day**
https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/bishnoi-movement/
https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/national-forest-martyrs-day-2025/
Indian civil-service exam coaching material. Supports the 2013 declaration of
11 September as National Forest Martyrs Day and its explicit link to Khejarli.
Gives 49 villages, against Wikipedia's 83 — hence the open question.

## Scholarly — identified, not yet consulted

**"Bishnoi: An Eco-Theological Movement", Journal of Vaishnava Studies**
https://ivsjournal.com/index.php/jvs/article/view/202
Paywalled. Would be the strongest single source for how scholars actually
characterise the tradition, and therefore for retiring the "original
environmentalists" framing in favour of defensible language.

**Census of India, 1961 — Bishnoi monograph**
Cited in the strategy discussion as recording Jambhaji's 1451 birth and 1536
death, the 20+9 etymology alongside the community's Vaishnava background, and the
progression from religious movement to distinct social community. Not reachable
from open search. Would upgrade the etymology and community-formation material.

**Mukam village survey, 1965**
A socio-economic study of actual Bishnoi village life — organisation, belief,
festivals, marriage, death customs, household economy, agriculture. The best
identified source for `/bishnoi/culture`, which is deliberately not in the first
six pages because this source is missing.

**Rajasthan High Court judgment (2026)** recounting the Khejarli narrative and the
reported royal response. Would carry unusual weight for claim 1 in the register —
a court reciting the history is a citable, non-community record.
