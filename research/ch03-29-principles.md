# Ch. 3 — The 29 Niyamas

**Feeds:** `/bishnoi/29-principles`
**Target queries:** 29 bishnoi principles · bishnoi rules · bishnoi niyamas · what are the 29 principles of bishnoi · why don't bishnoi wear blue

The highest search-value page in the first six. Also the one where the live site
is currently least accurate — see `claims-register.md` item 4.

---

## Source text — grade A

The principles derive from the **Shabadwani**, 120 *shabads* attributed to Guru
Jambheshwar, written in Nagri (Devanagari) script. The number 29 is not in
dispute; the enumeration and translation vary between sources, which is normal for
a text transmitted this way and should be said on the page.

---

## The 29, in the conventional enumeration — grade A

Categories as conventionally assigned. Four items carry no category in the source
we have; they are grouped under *personal discipline* below, which is our
inference and must be labelled as such on the page.

**Worship (4)**
1. Pray twice daily, morning and evening
2. Sing the evening aarti in praise of Vishnu
3. Perform yajna (havan)
4. Fast on Amavasya, the new moon
5. Worship and recite the name of Vishnu

> Note the arithmetic: the conventional "4 worship" count and the five items above
> do not reconcile cleanly, because sources differ on whether the Amavasya fast is
> worship or discipline. Flag on the page rather than silently choosing.

**Personal hygiene and purity (10)**
6. Observe a thirty-day period of separation after childbirth
7. Observe five days' separation during menstruation
8. Bathe daily, before sunrise
9. Keep to modesty, patience (kshama), contentment (santosha) and cleanliness (shaucha)
10. Use filtered water, filtered milk, and firewood cleaned of living creatures

**Social conduct (7)**
11. Speak truth, and speak it sincerely
12. Practise forgiveness and kindness (kshama, karuna)
13. Be merciful, sincerely
14. Do not steal (achaurya)
15. Do not condemn or criticise
16. Do not lie
17. Do not enter into disputes or conflict
18. Keep clear of lust, anger, greed and attachment (kama, krodha, lobha, moha)

**Environment and animals (8)**
19. Be merciful to all living beings, and love them
20. Do not cut green trees
21. Provide shelter for abandoned animals
22. Do not sterilise bulls; do not abandon male calves

**Personal discipline**
23. Cook your own food; be self-sufficient
24. Do not use or trade opium
25. Do not smoke or use tobacco
26. Do not take bhang, hemp or any intoxicant
27. Do not drink alcohol
28. Do not eat meat
29. **Do not wear blue attire**

## The indigo rule — grade A, and currently missing from the site

*Do not wear blue* is the rule most likely to make a reader stop, because the
reason is ecological rather than symbolic: indigo dye is extracted from the indigo
plant, and the extraction harms it. A prohibition on a **colour**, derived from a
prohibition on **harming a plant**, is the clearest single demonstration of how
far the tradition's ecological reasoning reaches into ordinary life.

It is absent from `src/components/Principles.tsx`. Restoring it is the highest-value
single content fix on the site.

## The framing the page must get right

The audit's strongest point about this material: the question is not *can the
visitor read all 29*, it is *does the visitor understand how these shaped Bishnoi
life*. A list is a reference document; the page needs to be an explanation.

Structure the page as **four questions, answered with the rules**, rather than as
four buckets of rules:

| Question | Answered by |
|---|---|
| How does a person live with animals? | the animal rules — shelter, no sterilisation, no meat |
| How does a person live with the land? | no green trees, no indigo, filtered water |
| How does a person live with others? | the seven social-conduct rules |
| How does a person live with themselves? | hygiene, fasting, the intoxicant rules |

That turns 29 rules into a culture, which is what the page is for.

## Open questions

1. Whether the Amavasya fast is conventionally worship or discipline — sources differ.
2. The original Rajasthani/Marwari terms for each niyama. Currently we have only
   English renderings, and the transliterated Sanskrit terms that appear in some
   of them (*kshama*, *santosha*, *shaucha*, *achaurya*) may be later glosses.
   The Shabadwani itself would settle this.
3. Whether the four-way categorisation is traditional or a modern editorial
   convenience. Until this is known, the page must present its grouping as its own.
