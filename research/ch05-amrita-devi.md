# Ch. 5 — Amrita Devi

**Feeds:** `/bishnoi/amrita-devi`
**Target queries:** amrita devi bishnoi · amrita devi · who was amrita devi · amrita devi bishnoi award · first tree hugger

A separate page from `/bishnoi/khejarli` on purpose. People search her name
directly, and the two pages answer different questions: hers is *who was this
person*, Khejarli's is *what happened that day*. Cross-link them; do not let
either restate the other.

---

## Established — grade A

- A woman of the village of Jehnad, later Khejarli, in Marwar
- September 1730: refused to let the Maharaja's party fell the khejri trees
- Rejected the bribe offered to stand aside, treating the offer as the insult
- Was killed first; three of her daughters followed and were killed after her
- 362 others died after them, 363 in total
- 11 September is now National Forest Martyrs Day in India, declared 2013
- The **Amrita Devi Bishnoi National Award** recognises individuals and rural
  community organisations showing exemplary courage or work in wildlife protection

## Tradition — grade B

- **Her daughters' names**: Asu, Ratni and Bhagu. Repeated consistently across
  community sources; Wikipedia's Khejarli article says only "three of her
  daughters". Attribute: *community tradition names her daughters as…*
- **Her words.** Renderings vary; the sense is that a head given for a tree is a
  fair exchange. Almost certainly later tradition, and none the weaker for it —
  but say so.

## The single best "past becomes present" fact on this site

The **Amrita Devi Bishnoi National Award** is the whole legacy argument in one
verifiable object: a woman killed in 1730 defending trees, whose name the Indian
state now attaches to its recognition of wildlife protection.

That is not community memory. It is a government instrument. It is the cleanest
answer available to *did this actually leave a mark*, and it belongs prominently
on this page — and nowhere near the business sections, where it would read as
borrowed credit.

---

## Why she needs her own page, editorially

The audit warned about the site's treatment of people — that a shared surname is
not an editorial reason for inclusion, and that a person belongs in the story when
their actions demonstrate the principles.

Amrita Devi is the case that defines the standard. She is in the story because
she is the moment the 29 niyamas stopped being instructions. Every other person
the site names should be able to meet the same test, which is the criterion to
apply when rebuilding "Faces of the Community".

She is also the correction to a second risk: that Bishnoi history reads as a
sequence of male religious authority with one woman inserted at the dramatic
moment. The record does not support that shape — women carried the practice
domestically, agriculturally and in transmission across generations — but the
current site does not show it either. This page should say what it can, and
`/bishnoi/culture` should carry the rest when the Mukam village survey is
available to support it.

## Open questions

1. Her age, family beyond the daughters, and whether her husband is named anywhere.
2. Whether any near-contemporary source names her at all, or whether the name
   itself enters the record later.
3. The award's founding date and administering ministry — currently we have its
   existence and purpose but not its provenance.
4. Whether the annual Khejarli commemoration predates the 2013 national
   designation, and by how long.
