# Ch. 6 — The community

**Feeds:** `/bishnoi` (hub)
**Target queries:** bishnoi community · who are the bishnoi · bishnoi people · bishnoi tribe · bishnoi rajasthan

The hub. Its job is to answer *who are the Bishnois* completely enough that a
reader needs nothing else, and to route to the other five pages for depth.

---

## Established — grade A

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A Vaishnava religious community following the 29 niyamas of Guru Jambheshwar |
| **Where** | Western Thar Desert of Rajasthan — around Bikaner, Jodhpur and Nagaur — with communities in Haryana, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat |
| **Structure** | Roughly 360 gotras, held to be of equal social standing |
| **Origins of membership** | Jat, Bania, Rajput, Khati, Gaena and other backgrounds; former caste names commonly set aside on joining |
| **Devotional character** | Vishnu as Narayana and Hari, alongside Lakshmi; within India's Sant devotional traditions |
| **Practice** | Strict vegetarianism; abstention from alcohol, tobacco, opium and hemp; protection of trees and animals |

## Disputed — grade C

**Population.** Sources conflict badly. Wikipedia's lead says roughly
**1,500,000**; the same article elsewhere says "as of 2010, an estimated
**600,000** followers". The live site's "several hundred thousand" is defensible
but weak.

Do not print a single figure. Print the range and its problem:

> Estimates vary widely, from around 600,000 to roughly 1.5 million, depending on
> whether the count is of religious adherents or of the wider community.

That sentence is more useful than either number and cannot be wrong.

## What the hub must not do

**Do not let the Bishnois become "the people who saved trees."** The conservation
story is the most visible part of the tradition and the most tempting to lead
with, and leading with it produces a reader who leaves thinking *Bishnoi =
environmentalists*. That is too narrow, and it is also the framing that makes the
company's use of the name look opportunistic.

The fuller answer to *what does it mean to be Bishnoi* covers community, ethics,
compassion, restraint, spiritual life, wildlife, custom, festival, migration,
craft and social conduct. Conservation is one expression of a philosophy about how
to live — with each other, with animals, and with the land.

**Do not mention the businesses.** The hub is about a people. The company appears
on the homepage and on `/businesses`, and the connection is drawn there. A reader
arriving from a search for "who are the bishnoi" should be able to read this page
end to end and never once feel sold to. That restraint is the appropriation
defence, and with no byline and no community reviewer it is the main one available.

## Structure

Answer the questions in the order a stranger would ask them, each with two or
three paragraphs and a link onward:

1. Who are the Bishnois? → this page
2. Where does the name come from? → `/bishnoi/name-and-origin`
3. Who was Guru Jambheshwar? → `/bishnoi/guru-jambheshwar`
4. What are the 29 principles? → `/bishnoi/29-principles`
5. What happened at Khejarli? → `/bishnoi/khejarli`
6. Who was Amrita Devi? → `/bishnoi/amrita-devi`
7. What are the Bishnois today? → this page, closing section

Section 7 is where the 245-year gap shows most. Until `/bishnoi/history` exists,
keep it to what is supportable: the community persists, practises, and is
recognised — the National Forest Martyrs Day designation and the Amrita Devi award
are the two hard facts available.

## Living tradition — grade A unless noted

- Blackbuck and chinkara move freely and safely through Bishnoi villages; hunting
  on community land is not tolerated
- Eight of the 29 principles concern animals and the environment
- **Orans** — community-protected stretches of native scrub, off-limits to cutting
  and grazing, functioning as sacred groves. Their existence is grade A; the claim
  that they measurably slow desertification is grade B and should be stated
  separately and more softly
- Contemporary Bishnoi conservationists including Khamu Ram Bishnoi and
  Radheshyam Bishnoi are recognised for grassroots wildlife protection work
- The Great Indian Bustard survives in the same desert; population figures move,
  so any number must be date-stamped and attributed

Festivals, already on the site and grade A: Janmashtami at Peepasar; the Bhadrapad
Amavasya fair at Mukam, the community's largest annual gathering; the Chaitra
Amavasya fair at Jambholav; monthly Amavasya fasting and satsang; the annual
Khejarli commemoration each September.

## Open questions

1. **The 245-year gap** — 1536 to 1730, and then 1730 to the present. This is the
   defining absence, and filling it is what would make the site a history of a
   people rather than a history of two famous days. It needs sources we do not
   have yet: colonial-period census descriptions, administrative records, the
   Mukam village survey.
2. Reconciling the population figures — which count, from which year, on what basis.
3. Migration: when, why, and to where. Currently the site says the community is
   "found further afield across northern India" and stops.
