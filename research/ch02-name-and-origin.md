# Ch. 2 — The name

**Feeds:** `/bishnoi/name-and-origin`
**Target queries:** bishnoi meaning · what does bishnoi mean · bishnoi surname origin · bishnoi vs vishnoi · is bishnoi a caste

A short page that answers one question completely. Low competition, clean intent,
and it is the question the site's own name most obviously raises.

---

## The two readings

### Reading 1 — *bis* + *noi* = twenty-nine — grade A as the mainstream reading

From the Rajasthani for twenty (*bis*) and nine (*noi* / *nau*), for the 29
principles. This is the derivation given by Wikipedia, by the community itself,
and by most scholarly writing on the subject.

Critically, **it is the community's own account of its name**, and the community
actively rejects the alternative — sources record that Bishnois did not accept
"Vishnoi" as a replacement spelling.

### Reading 2 — from Vishnu — grade C

Some Hindi-language authors, Hiralal Maheshwari among them, use *Vishnoi*,
"followers of Vishnu". Some scholars read that spelling as part of a broader
Hinduisation of community names after 1947, rather than as an older etymology.
Colonial-era gazetteer material is also reported to have recorded a Vishnu
association alongside the community's own 29-articles explanation.

The tradition is genuinely Vaishnava — the niyamas include worship of Vishnu as
Narayana and Hari — so the Vishnu reading is not baseless. It is a question of
which gave rise to the *name*.

---

## How to write it

Do not flatten this into "Bishnoi means twenty-nine". Do not flatten it into
"scholars disagree" either — that overstates the dispute and implicitly demotes
the community's own account to one opinion among many.

The honest shape is:

> The name is commonly derived from the Rajasthani *bis* (twenty) and *noi*
> (nine), for the twenty-nine principles — the explanation the community itself
> gives, and the one most sources follow. Some writers have instead connected the
> name to Vishnu, reflecting the tradition's Vaishnava character; the community
> has not accepted "Vishnoi" as a substitute spelling.

That is defensible, respectful, and more interesting than either version alone.

## Related, and worth the same page

**Is Bishnoi a caste?** — grade A, and a genuinely useful answer.

It began as a religious movement and became, over centuries, also a social
community. Followers came from Jat, Bania, Rajput, Khati, Gaena and other
backgrounds; on becoming Bishnoi they commonly set aside their former caste names.
There are roughly **360 gotras**, held to be of equal social standing.

The point the live site already makes well — that Bishnoi has always been a code
of conduct open to anyone who takes it up, not a bloodline — is well supported and
should be the page's second section.

The progression worth naming explicitly, because almost nothing online does:

> religious movement → community → social identity

not

> guru → Bishnoi caste
