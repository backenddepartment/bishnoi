## Established — grade A

| | |
|---|---|
| **Born** | 1451, at Peepasar, Nagaur district |
| **Founded the panth** | 1485, at Samrathal Dhora |
| **Preached** | 51 years thereafter, travelling widely |
| **Died** | 1536 |
| **Final rites / principal shrine** | Mukam, Nokha, Bikaner district |
| **Teachings** | 120 *shabads*, the **Shabadwani**, in Nagri script |
| **Also known as** | Jambhoji, Jambhaji, Jambheshwar Bhagwan |

The 1451 and 1536 dates are reported to appear in Census of India material as
well as in community sources, which is unusually good support for a 15th-century
religious figure.

## The framing correction this page exists to make

The site currently treats him as the origin point of a philosophy the company
later inherits — "the founder". That is the wrong emphasis twice over.

**First**, it collides with the company's own use of "founder" for Naresh Bishnoi.
Already fixed in the homepage eyebrow, but the page must not reintroduce it.

**Second**, and more importantly: he is not *the founder of a philosophy we use*.
He is **the person around whom a way of life formed**. The distinction the audit
drew is exactly right:

> not: founder → company
> but: Guru Jambheshwar → teachings → community → generations → living tradition

The page should end with the community, not with the company. Nothing on
`/bishnoi/guru-jambheshwar` should mention the businesses at all.

## The drought

The traditional account places his teaching against a severe 15th-century drought
in the Thar — and this is the part worth telling properly, because it explains why
the 29 principles look the way they do.

The response to scarcity was not conquest, migration or appeal to a ruler. It was
a set of rules about restraint: what you may cut, what you may kill, what you may
wear, what you may drink, how you treat an animal that is no longer useful to you.
A drought answered with a code of conduct.

Grade **B** — the drought framing is traditional account rather than documented
climatology. Say "tradition holds" and it is both accurate and undiminished.

## Sacred geography, in brief

The full treatment belongs on `/bishnoi/sacred-sites` (not in the first six).
This page needs only:

- **Peepasar** — birthplace; Janmashtami is observed there
- **Samrathal Dhora** — where the panth was established in 1485
- **Mukam** — final resting place and principal pilgrimage site; the Bhadrapad
  Amavasya fair is the community's largest annual gathering
- **Jambholav** — Chaitra Amavasya fair

Existing material in `src/components/Mukam.tsx` covers eight sites and lifts
across cleanly when that page is built.

## Modern legacy — grade A

**Guru Jambheshwar University of Science and Technology**, Hisar, Haryana,
established 1995, named in tribute. The university's own description of him — "a
saint environmentalist of the 15th century" — is already quoted correctly on the
site, and is a useful citation precisely because it is an institution, not a
community source, applying that description.

## Open questions

1. Whether any part of the biography beyond the dates is documented rather than
   traditional. The Census monograph is the place to look, and would let us
   separate the two cleanly on the page.
2. The content of the Shabadwani beyond the niyamas — 120 shabads is a substantial
   body of teaching and we currently know almost nothing about the other material.
3. His travels. "Preached for 51 years, travelling across India" is repeated
   everywhere and specified nowhere.
